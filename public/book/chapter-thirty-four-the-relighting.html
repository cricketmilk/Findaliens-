Part Four — The Keeping
Chapter Thirty Four — The Relighting

They came up at dusk, because the keepers ruled it so: not into noon, which would have blinded the deep born, but into the hour of candles, the threshold hour, the one the whole under country had kept for twenty years without a sky to hang it on.

The stair took the morning and the day. Cinder rode the last flight up on Wren's shoulder, and at the vent mouth she went out ahead of them into the moving air, and the sound she made off the mountain's face was one no living soul had heard from her, and every column below stopped to listen to it, and no one asked anyone what it was. Then the doors, and the cold, real cold, weather cold, and the smell the children had no name for and the old wept at, which was snow coming, and then the sky.

The deep born stood in the dusk with their heads back and did not speak. They had been raised on a chalk heaven, checked nightly against an old man's memory, and here was the original, enormous, unrolled from fold to fold of the world, and the elders watched the children do the thing they had all been waiting twenty years to watch: find their bearings in it. Eyes going down the vault, and along, and stopping, low in the west, where a bright star stood at the fold of the fells like a lamp in a window. A girl of nine, moor stock, born in the fourth gallery, raised on whitewax constellations, pointed at it and said its name aloud, short, like the coal settles, and the name went down the columns in one voice after another, and Aurelius stood on the high shoulder of the mountain with his catalogue proved and his hands at his sides, and let the sky be checked against him one last time, and passed.

The sky was correct. He had worried, privately, for twenty years, that he had misremembered a heaven. He had not. It was all where he had left it, the Empire's lies and the moor's memory and the one door, shut, latched, patient, and above the open fells it looked, for the first time in his long exile, less like a scatter than like a page held up to be read on.

They laid the fire bed on the old cairn shoulder, because the moor's sense of a beginning was as exact as its sense of an ending, and the order of the lighting went widdershins from the cairn, as it had gone since before the Empire had a name to forget. Brann's boy laid it, a grey man of forty now who had carried the order down and under and up again, and Thessine came to the bed with the glede box from Harrow Fell, twenty years of the same fire banked in it, carried through the dark of the world in an iron box like every fire her people had ever refused to lose. She was old now, keeper old, her exactness gone to bone, and she did not kneel, and did not hand the coal to Aurelius. She turned, and found the girl of nine who had named the star, and held out the box, and adjusted the child's grip on it once, gently, the way a woman corrects a grip on a spoon, and said, in the voice that had said every important thing in the history of the world once: "Widdershins. To the count. It is yours now, and it was always yours; we only kept it."

The child fed the coal in with two hands, grave as a coronation. The fire took. It stood up off the cairn into the first true dark, the mountain burning gold at its shoulder, and Brann's boy counted aloud, unhurried, the spoken eleven, and the whole nation of the deep turned west with the smoke, and waited, deep in the discipline of waiting, and on the eleventh second, out on the first far shoulder of the dark, small and stubborn and immediate, as if it had been standing by a laid bed for twenty years with a spill in its hand, an answer.

Then the next. Then, raggedly, wrongly, fires the count had not called, springing up at bad intervals on hills nobody had assigned, the surface's hidden keepers giving up their hiddenness all at once, overrunning the liturgy, and Veyra, at the fire bed's edge, sang the first verse of the Serrat setting and dropped her voice out from under it, her oldest craft, and the near hills took it from her in accents twenty years strange. Thessine watched the bad intervals multiply down both horizons and rendered the last finding anyone ever heard her deliver at a fire: "Let them be wrong. Wrong is teachable. Dark is what we could not work with."

And once, high in the north, crossing the wrong constellations on its dead patrol, the slow blind shape of the last of the empties passed over the whole burning chain, the final standing order of a fallen age still keeping a watch nobody had ever wanted, and the deep born looked up at it without fear, because they had been raised on the true account, and nobody below it knelt.

He stood at the cairn a long time, in the smoke and the answering light, the man the glamour could never read, Sol under an open sky at last, and let the accounting stand up around him at its full height, because some accounts do not shrink by being carried twenty years. Below the cairn, at the edge of the first fire's reach, the light found a steading roofless to the weather, one wall down, the door frame standing with no door in it, and behind it the orchard rows kept their lines under twenty years of bramble, planted by somebody who had counted on autumns. Nobody had cut the road to it since the rain. The Empire had not been saved. It had been survived. Between the fires on those horizons lay a scoured country that believed its murderers were its martyrs, that fought the Visitors in pageants and paid the Gilders for wards against a sky that had never once been the enemy, and every league of it would have to be walked, hearth by hearth, at the pace of the lighting, by people carrying a shelf's worth of terrible paper, cased and latched for the roads, every lid burned with the under children's mark, a small house with eyes beneath its floor, and ninety true songs, and the sentence the deep had kept alive against exactly this night. It was not the sky. It was men. It could have been stopped early, and was stopped late, and the difference between those two stoppings was the whole grey world between the fires. That was the map now. It ran both directions, the truth outward and the people home, and it had to be walked by hand, because the one mercy of the age's end was that there were no more instruments that could walk it for them.

Aldric came to him at the fire with the field case on his shoulder, the Mother's green glass throat wrapped against the cold, bound down the valley at first light for the border country and the ruin of a certain surgery, to reopen it above ground where it had begun, with terrace stock from the seed galleries packed against the cold, beebalm among it, the clock crop of a coast that would have to learn its own week again. There was nothing left to teach him and both of them knew it, so Aurelius closed the boy's hands, the man's hands, the physician's hands, over the case grip, and gave him the whole of the estate in the old words: "Finish the map, Magister. Both directions." And Aldric, who had crossed an age with a person who was not there folded and ready to wear and had never once needed to wear it below, took the title and the case and the road, and his face, in the firelight, did not even try to be steady, and had no need.

Wren stood the first surface watch that night, the wire moving through her fingers in figures that had crossed, by then, from craft into language, reading the answering fires the way her teacher had once read a basin's ripple. She had the count. The children of the deep all had the count; it was the first thing their world had taught them, before weather, before sky: eleven, hill to hill, a tended fire keeps time.

He sat down on the cold stone below the fire, an old man at last and content with the purchase, scarred hands at the top of a freed stair, and Cinder came down out of the dark she owed nothing to and took the stone beside him, unhooded, wrecked feathered, sovereign, and together they watched the horizons fill, fire by fire, wrong interval by wrong interval, the oldest sentence in the world being said slowly across a healing dark by everyone, in every accent, unlicensed, uncounted, alive.

Far down the western line, behind the fires, the bright star stood at the fold of the world, low, patient, keeping its watch over the whole relearning country: Lamp to the liars, Glede to the keepers, Latch to one reader only, and under all three names the same plain fact, which the Empire had buried, and the mountain had kept, and the children now carried up into the dark with their own hands.

Banked, the way the uplands banked a fire. Looking spent.

And not.
