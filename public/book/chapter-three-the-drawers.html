Part One — The Hollowing
Chapter Three — The Drawers

The old man's session went to procedure, but Aurelius did not. Subject: male, sixty years, the Ninth Province, unlicensed observance, which in his case had meant blessing boats. He confirmed the accounts, and the man drank, and the whole hour he did each correct thing knowing that correctness was all he was doing, because the chit sat in the pocket where the wire lived like a coal carried from someone else's fire, and his attention was on the chamber's corners, the seams of the door, the exact positions of his instruments, which had not moved, and which he checked twice. The man watched his own life arrive and arrive with mild interest, and at the verification he was asked what the sea was owed at a launching, and answered, pleasantly, that the sea was not owed anything, that the sea was water, and the protocol's vocabulary offered calm, and Aurelius wrote it.

He asked the man no question of his own. He noted that he had wanted to, and for the first time he did not enter the wanting in the ledger with no pages, because that ledger's whole safety had rested on a single arithmetic, that nobody knew to look for it, and the arithmetic had died at midday on a quartermaster's strip. He dismissed the old man to his veil memory and carried the stone up to the archive himself at a pace that was in no diagram. He had moved past wanting into arithmetic, and the arithmetic said the chit had not been left by a stranger. Strangers did not exist in the tower; that was the tower's entire premise. It was the last session of the week, because the week had another duty in it, and the duty was three days in the cold among the drawers, and he went down to it knowing that whoever had touched his one nerve had known, when they wrote, exactly where he would be standing when the words finished their work.

Twice a year the fourth floor audited the archive, under a sampling protocol he had written himself in the years when writing protocols had felt like building shelter, and so twice a year Aurelius spent three days in the cold doing arithmetic on the sleep of forty thousand souls.

This time he brought the boy.

"Crates," he said, when Aldric asked what for. "The audit receives the quarter's intake. Somebody carries the crates. The diagrams say an acolyte."

The diagrams did say that. It was even the reason.

The archive occupied three floors of the tower's cold heart, and the doors of it were brass, and past the doors the temperature dropped to the temperature of the deep fells and stayed there, because resonance keeps best at the cold of the country it was recorded in. The whole Empire's memory was stored at the temperature of its north.

Racks ran away under the lamps in aisles, and the aisles had streets' names, or what passed for them: indices, province and year, stamped on brass at every corner. The brass at the third corner was rubbed bright at thumb height and nowhere else, forty years of registrars taking the turn with a hand out, and the bright patch was shaped like a pear. He noticed it every audit. He had never once decided to. Each drawer was a hand's width. Each held one housing, one crystal, one card. The cards gave name, province, years of record, and the small notation the registrars called the closure, which said how the record had ended: surrendered at death, most of them, in the registrars' upright hand. The drawers went on. That was the fact of the place that no diagram conveyed. A man could walk, and keep walking, and the drawers went on, waist high, shoulder high, above reach, below knee, and every one of them had been a person's exact and entire weather, day over day, for the years the card declared.

The boy stopped five aisles in. Aurelius watched him do the thing that everyone did five aisles in, which was to begin, involuntarily, to count. The boy's hands hung still at his sides while the eyes went down the rack, and along, and up, and Aurelius could follow the multiplication moving in him, rack by aisle by floor, and then the multiplication stopped, and the boy stood in the presence of the sum.

"How far back," Aldric said.

"To the charter. Thirty years, wall to wall. Before that, partials. The Recording House kept death rolls and censuses; the stones begin with the charter."

"Every subject."

"At fifteen." He walked; the boy fell in beside the trolley. "A gift of the Emperor, in the charter years. People asked for them. There were waiting lists in the model provinces. You decorated it, you swore on it, you were buried having worn it your whole adult life, and the housing came here." He turned down the intake aisle. "Then the mandate. Different fitting, same crystal. The collars record more, and nobody waits in line for one, and none of that changes the drawers. The drawers only ever grow."

Aldric said nothing. His silences were becoming legible, which was itself a thing Aurelius filed and did not examine: this one was the dormitory silence, the one issued to children who are being taught a splendid fact and can find nothing in themselves that matches the splendor.

The quarter's intake stood at the receiving stage in sealed crates, come down from the chapter houses of the northern provinces with the wagons' cold still on them. The dead's stones. Surrendered at graveside, cased by the local Voices, shipped north with manifests. It was the plainest work in the Needle and Aurelius had never once done it without the sense that he was participating in a tide. They worked the morning: the boy unsealing, lifting each housing to the light, reading its card aloud; Aurelius verifying resonance against the manifest, logging decay, assigning the drawer. Name, province, years. Name, province, years. A weaver of the Second, forty years of record. A ferryman of the Fifth, fifty one years of record, the housing worn silver at one edge as brass wears when a thumb has visited it daily, and Aurelius held that one a moment longer than the log required, because somewhere in fifty one years of days there was a reason the man's thumb had gone to that edge, and the card did not give it, and the drawer would not ask.

Halfway through the second crate, Aldric's hand came up with something that was not a housing.

It was a shallow tray, packed by some chapter house registrar to travel atop the cased stones, and in it, tagged in the registrars' upright hand, lay the nonconforming attachments. The things removed. A chain replaced by braided horsehair, the horsehair coiled and tagged. A housing drilled, against every regulation, to take a silver ring. Dried flowers, pressed flat by the journey, tagged. A child's bone button on a loop of red thread, tagged, the tag reading only nonconforming, removed, Fourth Province, and the boy stood holding the tray of what people had done to the machines they were made to wear, all the small illegal tendernesses stripped off at death and shipped north as evidence of nothing, and he looked at Aurelius across it with a question he did not have words for.

"Fittings interfere with the seating of the drawer," Aurelius said. The sentence was true. He heard it be true the way the anneal monographs were true. "Log them as received. There's a case for them on the lower floor."

There was. A case, on the lower floor, of horsehair and rings and ribbon and buttons, decades of it, catalogued and kept, because the Chorus threw nothing away; the Chorus was constitutionally incapable of throwing anything away.

The boy logged the tray. His hands were steady. Aurelius had begun to understand what the steadiness cost, and what it was for, and that the dormitories had built better than they knew when they built a person who held still while things passed through him.

He sent Aldric up with the trolley at the fifth hour and finished the audit's last aisle alone, because the last aisle of the sampling run was the Fourth Province, current intake, and he had known it would be since the protocol assigned the samples, and he had let it stand.

The aisle was like every aisle. The drawer was like every drawer. Thessine. Fourth Province, northern district. Eleven years of record. And the closure, in the registrars' hand, in the space where the dead were marked surrendered: record closed by decree; subject continues.

Subject continues.

He stood in the cold with his audit slate and read the card twice, not because it was unclear.

The drawer's seating had a fault. That was what he told his hands as they opened it: the audit's business, seal and seating. The housing rode where he had left it after her session. Someone had serviced it since; the intake registrars serviced everything; and the plait of waxed cord that had ridden coiled around the brass, eleven years of some upland evening in it, somebody's hands braiding wax into thread by a fire he could see almost as clearly as if her stone had played it to him, was gone. Regulation chain lay in its place, bright, correct, seated.

The cord would be downstairs. Tagged, in the case, in the dark, with the buttons.

He performed the audit. Seal sound, resonance nominal, decay within curve. A stone in good order, keeping what it kept: the fells, the winters, the order of the lighting, all of it banked and none of it hers now; the woman it belonged to was in a convalescent cell eleven floors above learning to be grateful for a fever she had never had, and in the spring they would send her home, and the drawer would stay here, continuing, and between the woman and the drawer lay the whole of what he had built, and for the length of one breath in the cold he let himself know it. Not think it. He thought it daily; thinking was survivable. Know it, in the body, as one knows a stair has given way: the foot finding nothing, the fall not yet begun.

Then he closed the drawer, and initialed the slate, and his hand was level while it did it, which he noted, and did not admire.

On the lower floor, at the case of nonconforming attachments, he spent four minutes locating the tag. Fourth Province, northern district, cord, waxed, removed at service. The case kept its own register, a line waiting for every tag withdrawn, and he stood over the open page with the pen in reach and his hand not reaching, listening to the stair behind him with the whole of his back, and then closed the case on its unwritten line and left with the plait of waxed cord coiled in the pocket where the wire lived, and the chit with its five words, and it came to him on the stairs, with the sound of his own steps going up through the cold, that he was assembling something in that pocket, article by article, and that a man of his training could probably have named it, and he declined to.

At the brass doors the visit ledger took his signature, hour in, hour out, purpose of entry. Audit, he wrote, in the space for purpose, in a tower where every record was a record and every reader was read. A spike of petitions stood at the counter's end, kin asking hours with their kept dead, every one stamped referred, which meant the chapter houses, which meant rates. Somewhere below, in the long rooms, forty thousand held breaths kept their cold. He initialed the line, and noticed his own name standing in it, Aurelius Sol, senior of the fourth floor, and thought, in the register the day had worn smooth: there is a drawer being kept on me as well.
