Chapter Twenty Six — The Cathedral

The sun set on the first evening of the ended world, and nobody watched it go down, because the Empire was still listening to its dead.

The tower worked through the dusk in a state he had no word for and finally, watching a hall prefect weep quietly over a resonance log, identified: triumph, worn as reverence. The great work was running. The basins carried uptake figures from nine provinces, and the figures sang: the kneeling holding steady in the squares through the cold, the fill seating in the tuned and the thinned at rates the models called complete, whole districts of the Concord passing, hour by hour, into the serenity the sermons had spent a century calling peace and the annexes called, in the one honest word of the entire enterprise, capacity. On the terraces the Voices embraced. In the refectories the acolytes were given honey wine. The Chorus of the Lasting Concord had ended the world on schedule, and the world, by every instrument the Chorus owned, was grateful.

Aurelius was ordered to the archive at the eighth hour, and went, warrant in hand, the trusted certifier descending into his own long rooms to monitor the holdings under load, and so it was that he stood alone among the racks on the night of the Rending and heard what the drawers had become.

The cathedral was singing itself to death.

He had always known it, the way one knows a figure without letting it resolve: the beloved dead speaking in a hundred thousand ears tonight were distilled from the archive's holdings, the cadences strip mined live from the surrendered stones, forty thousand dead lives being played at once, at full draw, through the grid, as fuel. The long rooms roared with it, a sound like a choir pressed past its compass, every drawer lit faintly from within, the whole cold nation of the dead conscripted in one night to impersonate itself to its own grandchildren, and under the draw, one by one, the oldest stones were failing. He walked the aisles and heard them go: a thin crystalline note, a drawer going dark, fifty one years of a ferryman burned through in six hours of being his own descendants' comforter, the housing cracking, and then silence where a held breath had been.

They had not modeled conservation. Why would they. The dead were feedstock; the archive had always been a fuel depot wearing a reliquary's manners; and the Chorus was spending it the way it spent everything, entirely, because after tonight there would be no need of archives. He stood in the dark with the dead breaking around him like surf and could do nothing for them at all, nothing, except the one thing he did do, which was to stay: to walk the aisles all night, drawer to drawer, as the housings cracked, with his hand laid on the brass of each one that failed, four seconds, and the card read aloud into the roaring dark, "A weaver, of the Second, forty years," "A midwife, of the Seventh, fifty three years," the whole liturgy he had left, spoken in a steady voice to an audience of none, so that of the forty thousand conscripted dead of the Concord, some hundreds, at least, did not go out of the world uncounted.

And in the far aisles, where the last unshipped tranche of the closed by decree stood waiting for wagons that the Rending had made irrelevant, the other sound: silence, held. Fourteen of the four hundred and six remained at the Needle, and the flood was at them too, their own borrowed dead knocking with the rest, and fourteen small doors stood shut in the roaring cathedral, singing their eleven underneath it all, and every time he passed that aisle in the breaking dark he steadied on it the way a man in weather steadies on a lit window.

The reports began to cross his desk after midnight, because he was the certifying instrument, and the instrument was asked, with the halls' full trust, to explain the anomalies.

Uptake below model: the western foothills, the fen districts, scattered lanes in nine provinces, ninety one addresses flagged in the collation as nonreceptive, held over for review. A warehouse in the Fifth reporting a tranche of holdings inert under load, requesting resonance inspection. The glamour's forecast deviations, small at dusk, compounding by the hour, and the night clerks bringing each one to the senior alchemist with the anxious deference of men handing a fever to a physician. "It will be the cold," the youngest said, at the third report, wanting it to be the cold, holding the page out like a child holding out a cut. "Magister. It is the cold?" "Enter it as variance," the physician said, kindly, in the voice of forty years of findings, and the boy went away comforted, carrying the lie like a dressing, and Aurelius certified, in his exact hand, plausible causes: cold snap variance, freight settling, instrument drift. Every certification true somewhere in the world. Every one a lie where it stood. He was buying hours with his own credibility, spending the last coin of his whole credit at the rate the night demanded, and he paid it out steadily and did not count the remainder, because the remainder was not going to matter after the second act.

Word from the west came at the fourth hour, by the only channel the Rending could not read: fire.

He saw it from the archive's high vent windows on his round, and went up to the terrace parapet to read it whole: the beacon chain alive along the western horizon, but changed, the steady vigil rhythm broken up now into patterned bursts, long and short, fires occulted and shown, occulted and shown, wick code scaled up to hilltops, the web talking to itself across the province in the dark. Old Cade's baskets had gone out to forty hills, and the hills were reporting: doors held. all doors held. And then, walking the horizon line, hill to hill, a longer message, patient, repeated, the same figures over and over, and he stood in the freezing dark with his hands on the stone and worked it out, and it was not operational traffic at all. It was for him. Thessine, somewhere on her fell with a signal fire and a shutter board, sending east across a hundred miles of ended world the one dispatch she judged the night required:

The waking goes house to house. The names hold. Teach where they put you.

His own sentence, carried a year, sent back to him across the dark of the Rending like a coal in an iron box. In the lanes below the hills, he knew now, the counter circuit was running inside the catastrophe itself: the restored walking village to village through the flood with tapers, the wardsill bearers waking under the fill's own pressure the way she had woken, the star names said aloud over the panic in a voice that corrected gently and did not apologize, and every door the anneal had once unhinged now answering, one by one, in the oldest grammar: who is it. Who is it. Who is it.

The sky began to give at the fifth hour.

He felt it before he saw anything, and what he felt was not on any instrument: the groping half sense in him that had hunted a figure in this world's sky for forty years and never found the door, went quiet the way it went quiet at the beacon fires, and then, slowly, terribly, began to point. Not west, at the hills. Up.

The night was clear, hard winter black, the stars in their wrong constellations. And at the zenith, faint past faintness, where no aurora belonged and no aurora was, the dark had begun to grain. Pattern, at the very threshold of seeing: the sky's blackness taking on a weave, a figure, and his eyes, which had reached for a figure in that scatter since a cold field forty years ago, locked to it and could not be argued down. The grid roaring at full draw. The beacons burning on every high place. A hundred thousand collars singing. The eclipse's wound still cooling in the heavens. Every threshold in the Empire lit and working in one night, and behind the manufactured Rending, drawn by it or freed by it or simply noticing it, the true one had begun, very quietly, to lean against the worn places of the world.

Below him in the tower the triumph went on. The instruments read nothing. The cantus had no page for this; the annexes had no annex; the thing pressing was not even, and not manufactured, and did not knock, because it was not a story seeking entry. It was the far side of every fire the moor had ever lit, coming up like a reflection through still water toward its own surface.

Cinder came off the aerie ledge in the black before dawn and did not hunt. She rose instead, ring upon ring above the tower's crown, higher than he had ever watched her fly at night, a speck against the graining dark, and she cried up there, twice, a sound he had never once heard her make in forty years, and it was not alarm and it was not rage, the only two musics she had ever owned. It was answer.

Then she came down the sky in one long fall and took the parapet rail beside him, wild eyed, feathers pressed flat, and looked not at him but up, and made the sound a third time, low now, at the edge of hearing, at the exact pitch, he realized with the hair rising on his arms, of a struck stone. Of resonance finding its pair.

"I know," Aurelius said quietly, into the wind, to the bird, to the graining sky, to whatever leaned. "I feel it too."

The finding he entered in the black before dawn, at the rail, with the cathedral breaking below him and the hills talking fire behind him and the first true morning of the ended world an hour off, he wrote without ornament, because the hour did not lend itself to any:

The Chorus had written a counterfeit Rending, and the counterfeit was running to schedule. And behind it, unscripted, unpriced, leaning on the thin worn places with the patience of the real, the original had begun to arrive, and no one in the halls had an instrument that could see it, because their instruments had been built, every one, to read only what men had made.

Something that was in nobody's cantus was leaning against the sky. And the sky was starting to give.
