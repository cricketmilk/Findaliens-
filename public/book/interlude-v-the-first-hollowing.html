Part Three — The Rending
Interlude V — The First Hollowing

The fifth impression has ridden against his ribs for three days, in the inner pocket, beside the taper. It is the oldest sealed and the only one wait marked, in his own cipher, in a younger hand: do not. It does not need the Mother's throat, and never did. It has been playing itself under everything since the night it was made, at the floor of him, below the ledgers, and all he does now, on the lit stage, in the held breath of the world, is stop refusing to listen.

It goes like this.

He is one year into the tower, and the work is glorious, and the funding is bottomless, and the valley is being surveyed for the mews, and the letter from Marrow comes in her terrible handwriting, three lines, because she writes like she splints: They have been to the surgery. They want the casebooks and the process. I have told them what I think of institutions that shop for mercy with soldiers. Come take your equipment before they impound it, boy.

He goes south believing it is an administrative problem. He is one year into the warmth; he believes the Chorus has administrative problems; he believes he has standing. He arrives to find the surgery sealed, the casebooks crated, and Marrow charged. The charges are read to him by a district Voice with a kind face: obstruction of chartered inquiry, misappropriation of processes deemed vital to the Concord, instruction of unlicensed persons, and, appended in the dry way that he will later come to know as the true signature of the halls, persistent dissonance. She had treated the collared and the uncollared alike and kept no registers of either. She had told the acquisition clerks, in front of witnesses, that the gentling was a physician's art and would go to the halls over her body. She had, the kind faced Voice explains regretfully, left the Chorus very little room.

The sentence is the chamber. Not as punishment; the Voice is careful about this, and the care is the horror: as resolution. A dissonant element, resolved into harmony. The procedure is new, the first sanctioned application of the refined process outside the laboratories, and the halls, in recognition of his standing and his intimacy with the art, and in what Ignis will describe to him that evening, warmly, with his hand on his shoulder, as a mercy obtainable by no other channel, offer him the choice they have engineered him into: the provosts can resolve her their way, which is a rope, or the art can resolve her his way, which is a chair, and a chalice, and the finest hands she ever trained.

He believes the vacancy will be temporary. He needs this sentence entered in the record entire, because it is the load bearing lie of his life: he believes, with the whole force of a mind that has never yet been wrong about a mechanism, that what the process empties he will one day learn to refill; the reversal is already half sketched in his private notes; give him five years, three, and he will come back for her and pour her home. Ignis does not tell him this. Ignis merely does not correct it. The redirection at its masterwork: no lie anywhere, and a man walks into the worst room of his life on a bridge of his own hope.

The early procedure takes six hours, and the subject is awake for all of them.

Marrow watches him prepare the chalice the way she watched everything, judging soundness by light. She is sixty one years old, iron haired, her knuckles thick from forty years of other people's bones, and she is not afraid, which he files, that day, under courage, and will refile, years later, under something worse: she is not afraid because she is with him. Because of every pair of hands in the Concord, these are the ones she trusts. That is the mechanism the halls have built and he cannot see it yet: they have not asked him to betray her. They have asked him to be trusted at her, which no provost could be.

She talks through the first hours, because of course she talks. She narrates her own emptying like a case. The taste is nothing. Note that. Seven passes, you said; you always were vain about your sevens. And later, as the softening takes: Interesting. The meaning goes before the memory. The widow Casse in reverse. Write that down, boy, don't sit there dripping, one of us should be working. And later still, quieter, the edges of her going wide and calm: I can see the whole map from here. You were right about the architecture. Wrong about the door. There's always a door.

Near the fifth hour, in the low country between herself and the vacancy, with her voice already acquiring the terrible lightness he will spend the rest of his service hearing in other mouths, Marrow says the last weighted thing she will ever say, and she says it in her flat, unimpressible, splint setting voice, as an instruction, because she has never in her life said anything to him that was not either a finding or an order:

"None of this lands on your account, whatever they make you sign. Their ledger, not yours. But hear me, because I am only going to be able to say it once." Her eyes find his and hold, the last of the weight in her gathered up behind them like a household carried to a doorway. "The map runs both directions. I told you that the first week and you have spent your good years walking it one way. Finish it, boy. However long it takes, whatever it costs, whoever forbids it. Finish the map. Both directions. And when you have," and here, at the very lip of the vacancy, the flat voice does the one thing he has never once heard it do, which is soften, "come back down the map and find me on it. I'll keep."

The sixth hour empties her.

The verification, when he can speak to perform it, is textbook. She answers pleasantly. The eggs, the garden, the forty years of bones: present, weightless, filed. Her hands, folded in her lap, stir once as he watches, and arrange themselves around an invisible wrist, two fingers laid along where a pulse would run, the body keeping the physician after the halls had taken the woman, and the record he seals that night, wait marked do not, ends there, because the man sealing it could not carry the impression one second further and never has.

She lived seven years, attuned, in the border town, in the house behind the surgery. He was not permitted contact; the halls were gentle about it, and firm; the resolved are not to be unsettled. The web of her old patients, he learned much later, looked after her without being asked, milk on the sill, wood in the store, the valley quietly keeping its keeper. She tended the garden the whole seven years. Beds in rows, weedless, exact. Nothing planted that fed anyone. She died in it, in the spring of the seventh year, between the rows, of her heart, and the report reached him at the mews as a single line in a district digest, resolved subject, natural conclusion, no action, and he filed it, and did not close the file, and the reversal was finished seven years too late, and the first person it was ever owed to is the one drawer in the Concord that will never sing.

That is the stone. That is the whole of the stone.

He stands on the lit stage of the ended world with his eyes closed and the impression completing itself for the first time in fourteen years, and above him the sky is open, and behind the sky is the far side, pattern and rest and the whole country he was thrown out of, the door home, unlatched at last, three breaths away, and the stone finishes and leaves him, as she meant it to, as she engineered it to with the last weight in her, holding not the guilt, which is unpayable and always was, but the order. Her voice, flat as a splint, from the far side of fourteen years and one vacancy:

Finish the map. Both directions. Come back down it.

I'll keep.

He opens his eyes. The door in the sky stands open. And Aurelius, the man the Song could not find, turns his back on it, and faces the Empire, and reaches into his coat.
