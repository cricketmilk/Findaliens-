How to read this

  • Each node is an editorial dossier: a crisis window, the named insiders, the transaction, the mechanism, who gained, who lost, and the structural legacy. The wording is the Gazette's own.
  • The nodes carry no citations of their own. The sourced layer is The Web, where every approved fact and connection about a name carries its source, and the Handoff Ledger, where public programs that ended and the private successors that took over are graded by documented evidence. Where a node's names have research on The Web, its case file links there.
  • Lineages group nodes that the atlas reads as one continuing pattern. Names that recur are counted mechanically from the insider lists: a short name contained in a longer one ("Thiel" in "Peter Thiel") is merged into the longer.
  • Corn ratings on the front page measure how contested a story is. Here the equivalent is simpler: an entry on this tape is a claim about history, and the Web is where it is tested.
