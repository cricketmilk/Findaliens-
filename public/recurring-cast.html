1953–1954 · OPERATION AJAX AND THE CONSORTIUM
CIASISANGLO-IRANIAN / BP

TWO INTELLIGENCE SERVICES OVERTHROW A GOVERNMENT; THE OIL COMPANY GETS 40%

Iran nationalized the Anglo-Iranian Oil Company in 1951. The CIA and British intelligence planned and ran the August 1953 coup that removed Prime Minister Mosaddeq, acknowledged in the CIA's own 2013 release. Under the 1954 agreement, Anglo-Iranian (BP's predecessor) took 40% of the new consortium, Shell 14%, five American majors 7% each. This is the first joint: state intelligence acting for a company's assets, and the company paid in shares.

1973–1975 · THE PRICE SHOCK AND THE SECRET TREASURY ARRANGEMENT
TREASURY · SIMON · KISSINGERSAMA · FAISAL · FAHDTHE SHAH

THE SHAH PUSHES THE PRICE UP; SAUDI ARABIA IS OFFERED A CONFIDENTIAL WAY TO BUY TREASURIES OUTSIDE THE AUCTIONS

The FRUS energy-crisis volumes record the October 1973 embargo, the Shah's December 1973 Tehran decision that took the posted price to $11.60, and Kissinger's memoranda on the Shah's interest in high prices. In July 1974 Treasury Secretary Simon told Nixon he wanted "some commitment" from the Saudis to buy securities; the GAO reported in 1979 that a noncompetitive "add-on" purchase facility "was first offered to Saudi Arabia," that Treasury "made special commitments of financial confidentiality to Saudi Arabia," and that the Joint Commission was funded "almost entirely from the Saudi Arabian Trust Account in the U.S. Treasury." Treasury disclosed the Saudi holdings figure only in 2016. This is the documented mechanism that turns the consortium era into the Gulf-money era. What is not documented is any "oil priced in dollars for protection" pact: no released document states one.

"The noncompetitive 'add-on' purchase facility for Treasury securities was first offered to Saudi Arabia" … Treasury "made special commitments of financial confidentiality to Saudi Arabia."GAO EMD-79-45, June 1979 · the sourced record on The Web →

ONE BANK: GULF OWNERS, A SECRET AMERICAN BANK, CIA ACCOUNTS, IRAN ARMS, AND A REGULATOR THAT WITHHELD

BCCI secretly bought First American Bankshares through nominees fronted by Adham, with Clark Clifford and Robert Altman assisting from 1977. The CIA knew "more about BCCI's goals and intentions concerning the U.S. banking system than anyone else in government" by 1985 and used the bank for its own accounts. Abu Dhabi's ruler owned 77%. The bank was closed in July 1991; Adham pleaded guilty in 1992 and paid $105 million. This is the densest joint in the whole record: five of the six countries in the question, in one institution, with court-grade sourcing.

WHERE THAT LEAVES IT. Thirteen joints, each documented, four gaps, each marked. What repeats is the method: intelligence services and the financiers who serve them, Gulf and British money, a bank that asks no questions, and a public case that the record later contradicts. What the record cannot do is turn the repeats into a single chain with a hand on it. The Gazette's rating is four ears: the joints are solid, the thread through them is the reader's to draw, and the gaps are where the next research belongs.
