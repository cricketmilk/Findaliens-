🗂️ WHAT THE RECORD SAYS ABOUT SAUDI ARABIA AND 9/11

FORENSIC SCOPE: This page does one thing: it lays out, in order, every official finding, released document and court ruling on Saudi Arabia's relationship to the September 11 attacks, quoting each one and linking its source. It does not add a conclusion of its own. The reader gets the Commission's words, the FBI's words, the judge's words and the Kingdom's words, and decides. The research behind it is a sourced record in The Web, where each item carries its publisher and URL.
ON THE RECORD
  • Fifteen of the nineteen hijackers were Saudi nationals.
  • A Saudi consular official in Los Angeles and a man the FBI later called a paid cooptee of Saudi General Intelligence dealt with two hijackers on arrival in California.
  • The 28 pages say some hijackers "received support or assistance from individuals who may be connected to the Saudi Government."
  • A 2017 FBI memo says the earlier allegations about al-Bayoumi's intelligence ties were "not confirmed at the time of the 9/11 Commission Report" and that new information "confirms these allegations."
  • In August 2025 a federal judge found the Kingdom's explanations "either self-contradictory or not strong enough" to defeat the inference that it employed those two men to assist the hijackers, and sent the families' case toward trial.
NOT FOUND BY ANY INQUIRY
  • That the Saudi government as an institution, or senior Saudi officials, funded or directed the attacks. The Commission found "no evidence" of it.
  • That al-Bayoumi, al-Thumairy and al-Jarrah knowingly conspired in the attack. The FBI's own closing material on Operation Encore says the evidence "did not establish" it.
  • A finding of liability against the Kingdom. The 2025 ruling lets the case proceed; it decides nothing on the merits.
  • Any link from a Saudi role to the decision to invade Iraq. No source in the record connects the two.
22 JULY 2004 · THE 9/11 COMMISSION

THE COMMISSION: "NO EVIDENCE THAT THE SAUDI GOVERNMENT AS AN INSTITUTION" FUNDED AL QAEDA

The National Commission on Terrorist Attacks Upon the United States attributed the attacks to al Qaeda and, on money, drew a line that has shaped every argument since: no evidence of funding by the state or by senior officials acting individually, alongside a description of Saudi Arabia as the place al Qaeda raised money from individuals and charities, some with significant government sponsorship.

"Saudi Arabia has long been considered the primary source of Al Qaeda funding, but we have found no evidence that the Saudi government as an institution or senior Saudi officials individually funded the organization."9/11 Commission Report, chapter 5 · GovInfo PDF ↗
"We have seen no credible evidence that he [Bayoumi] believed in violent extremism or knowingly aided extremist groups. … After exploring the available leads, we have not found evidence that Thumairy provided assistance to the two operatives."9/11 Commission Report, chapter 7 · chapter 7 PDF ↗
RECORD: ⚖️ OFFICIAL FINDING #COMMISSION #15_OF_19
15 JULY 2016 · THE 28 PAGES

THE 28 PAGES: "INDIVIDUALS WHO MAY BE CONNECTED TO THE SAUDI GOVERNMENT"

The section of the 2002 congressional Joint Inquiry withheld in full for thirteen years. It names Omar al-Bayoumi, a U.S. resident who "may have been a Saudi intelligence agent" with money from a Saudi defense contractor, and Fahad al-Thumairy, a Saudi consular official and imam of a mosque two hijackers visited. The Inquiry made no final determination on the information's reliability, and the committee releasing it called the contents unverified leads.

"While in the United States, some of the September 11 hijackers were in contact with, and received support or assistance from, individuals who may be connected to the Saudi Government."Joint Inquiry, part four, declassified by the House Intelligence Committee · intelligence.house.gov ↗
"This section does not put forward vetted conclusions, but rather unverified leads that were later fully investigated by the Intelligence Community."Chairman Devin Nunes, on release · CNN ↗
"The 9/11 Commission and several government agencies, including the CIA and the FBI, have investigated the contents of the '28 Pages' and have confirmed that neither the Saudi government, nor senior Saudi officials, nor any person acting on behalf of the Saudi government provided any support or encouragement for these attacks."Saudi Ambassador Abdullah Al-Saud, July 2016 · Royal Embassy of Saudi Arabia ↗
RECORD: 📄 RELEASED DOCUMENT #28_PAGES #BAYOUMI #THUMAIRY
28 SEPTEMBER 2016 · JASTA

CONGRESS OVERRIDES A VETO, 97–1 AND 348–77, SO THE FAMILIES CAN SUE

The Justice Against Sponsors of Terrorism Act narrowed foreign sovereign immunity for terrorist attacks on U.S. soil. It became law over President Obama's veto, the first override of his presidency, and reopened the families' claims against the Kingdom that a judge had dismissed in 2015 under the Foreign Sovereign Immunities Act.

"The Senate voted 97-1, and the House followed the same day, 348-77."NPR, 28 September 2016 · via MPR News ↗ · ABA Journal ↗
RECORD: 🏛️ PUBLIC LAW #JASTA
11 SEPTEMBER 2021 · OPERATION ENCORE, RELEASED UNDER EXECUTIVE ORDER 14040

THE FBI'S OWN CASEFILE: "DEEPLY INVOLVED" IN HELPING TWO HIJACKERS

Executive Order 14040 ordered a declassification review. The first document out, a 16-page FBI electronic communication from April 2016 that had been withheld in full in the litigation, summarized Operation Encore, the casefile split off from the main investigation whose subjects were al-Thumairy, al-Bayoumi and Musaed al-Jarrah. Reporting on it described al-Bayoumi as deeply involved in travel assistance, lodging and financing for two hijackers, and al-Thumairy tasking an associate to help them. More than 400 documents followed.

"An electronic communication dated April 4, 2016, from the subfile investigation described in chapter V of the 2015 Report of the Congressionally-directed 9/11 Review Commission to the Director of the FBI."FBI release note, The Vault · vault.fbi.gov ↗ · NPR ↗ · CBS ↗
RECORD: 📄 RELEASED DOCUMENT #EO_14040 #OPERATION_ENCORE
2017 MEMO, RELEASED 2022 · THE FBI ON AL-BAYOUMI

"A COOPTEE OF SAUDI GENERAL INTELLIGENCE," PAID MONTHLY, ROUTED THROUGH THE AMBASSADOR

A heavily redacted 2017 FBI memo states that al-Bayoumi received a monthly payment as a cooptee of Saudi General Intelligence, routed through then-Ambassador Prince Bandar bin Sultan, and that the allegations about his intelligence ties were not confirmed when the Commission reported. A separate memo assessed, on source reporting, a 50/50 chance he had advance knowledge. These are FBI assessments, not findings of fact, and the same casefile closed without establishing a knowing conspiracy.

"The earlier allegations about his Saudi intelligence ties were not confirmed at the time of the 9/11 Commission Report. The above information confirms these allegations."FBI memo, 2017, declassified under EO 14040 · CBS News ↗ · FBI Vault PDF ↗
"The evidence did not establish that Thumairy, Bayoumi, and Al-Jarrah knowingly conspired to assist the AQ hijackers in furtherance of the 9/11 attack."Operation Encore closing material, FBI · ProPublica ↗
RECORD: 🔍 FBI ASSESSMENT #GID #BANDAR
2024 · THE CAPITOL VIDEO, UNSEALED IN THE FAMILIES' CASE

A VIDEO SEIZED IN 2001, A FULL COPY THE FBI DID NOT SHARE, AND A "SQUIRREL ON THE WHITE HOUSE LAWN"

Evidence unsealed in the litigation included a video seized from al-Bayoumi's Birmingham home in 2001, showing him touring Washington and filming the Capitol's architecture, entrances and security guards while referring to a plan. ProPublica reported the FBI had not shared a full copy with its own field agents or the families; British police provided it. The Kingdom's lawyers called it a tourist video, and said a notepad sketch of a plane was a child's maths assignment.

Attorneys for the kingdom dismissed the Capitol "casing" film as a "tourist video that includes footage of artwork, flowerbeds, and a squirrel on the White House lawn."ProPublica, 2024 · ProPublica ↗ · 60 Minutes ↗
RECORD: 📄 UNSEALED EVIDENCE #CAPITOL_VIDEO
28 AUGUST 2025 · SOUTHERN DISTRICT OF NEW YORK

THE JUDGE: THE KINGDOM'S EXPLANATIONS ARE "EITHER SELF-CONTRADICTORY OR NOT STRONG ENOUGH"

Judge George B. Daniels, who had dismissed the claims in 2015 before JASTA, denied Saudi Arabia's renewed motion to dismiss the consolidated families' case, In re Terrorist Attacks on September 11, 2001. He found the allegations met JASTA's jurisdictional requirements and that the evidence supported a reasonable inference that the two men's employment was more than their job titles suggested. The ruling lets the case proceed toward trial. It is not a finding of liability.

Saudi Arabia's explanations were "either self-contradictory or not strong enough to overcome the inference that" the Kingdom had employed two individuals, Omar al-Bayoumi and Fahad al-Thumairy, to assist the hijackers.Opinion as reported · CNN ↗ · Transnational Litigation Blog ↗ · docket, MDL 1570 ↗
"No evidence has ever emerged to indicate that the Saudi government or its officials had previous knowledge of the terrorist attack or were in any way involved in its planning or execution."Royal Embassy of Saudi Arabia · CBS News ↗
RECORD: ⚖️ COURT RULING, CASE PROCEEDS KINGDOM: DENIES #JASTA #MDL_1570
WHERE THAT LEAVES IT. Two things are true at once in the record. State employees of Saudi Arabia helped two hijackers in California, and the FBI's own memo says one of them was paid by Saudi intelligence through the ambassador. And no inquiry, including that same FBI casefile, has found that the Kingdom ordered, funded or knowingly joined the attack. The gap between those two sentences is exactly what the families' lawsuit is trying to close, and as of the 2025 ruling it is going to trial. The Gazette's corn rating for the page is four ears: the facts are documented, the conclusion is contested, and the reader should hold both.
