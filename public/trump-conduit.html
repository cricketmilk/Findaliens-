🏛️ TRUMP AS CONDUIT: WHAT THE RECORD SAYS

FORENSIC SCOPE: The question put to the record was whether Donald Trump has been a crash conduit or a wealth transfer for foreign interests, in the sense this site uses on Crash Conduits: someone standing in a crisis window who converts other people's money into durable private holdings. The answer below is built from thirty-one research files, each item quoted and linked to its source. Three crisis windows are marked in gold on the rail: 1990, 2008 and 2020–21. What no inquiry has found is listed first, so the reader knows the ceiling before the floor.
ON THE RECORD
  • A New York court found him liable for a decade of inflated statements of financial condition submitted to Deutsche Bank and others; the appeals court vacated the $464.6M penalty as an excessive fine and left the liability findings and the leadership bans in place.
  • In 1990 his banks put him on a $450,000-a-month allowance; in July 2008 a Russian billionaire paid $95M for a Palm Beach house he had bought for $41M four years earlier.
  • His casino paid a $10M FinCEN penalty for willful, repeated Bank Secrecy Act violations with citations going back to 2003.
  • A convicted racketeer and FBI cooperator carried a Trump Organization business card and brokered a Trump Tower Moscow letter of intent Trump signed during the 2016 campaign, per the Mueller report.
  • Saudi Arabia's sovereign fund put $2B into his son-in-law's new firm six months after office, over its own screening panel's objection; the firm has since collected $157.5M in fees from foreign investors.
  • His businesses took at least $7.8M from 20 foreign governments while he was president, per a House committee staff report; his 2025 and 2026 disclosures show tens of millions in Gulf licensing income and over half a billion in crypto tokens.
  • An Abu Dhabi fund settled $2B in his family's five-week-old stablecoin; he pardoned the founder of the exchange that received it.
NOT FOUND BY ANY INQUIRY
  • That any foreign government paid Trump for a specific official act. The senators' and committee letters allege it; none has been adjudicated.
  • That the 2008 Palm Beach sale was anything other than a sale. Both men deny knowing each other; Treasury never answered the senators' 2018 letters publicly.
  • That Deutsche Bank's Russian mirror-trade business and its Trump lending were connected. The record ties them only by the institution.
  • That Qatar's sovereign fund played any part in the 666 Fifth Avenue rescue. Brookfield and QIA both deny it; the Senate Finance Committee asked twice and was declined.
  • That the Binance pardon was bought. The White House, Binance and Zhao deny any deal; the Senate letter asks the question; no finding exists.
CRISIS WINDOW · 1990–1992 · THE BANK ALLOWANCE
CITIBANK AND THE LENDER GROUPNEW JERSEY REGULATORS

$3.4 BILLION OF DEBT, $832 MILLION PERSONALLY GUARANTEED, AND A $450,000-A-MONTH ALLOWANCE FROM THE BANKS

The first window is his own. In June 1990 he missed an interest payment on the Castle casino; the banks lent him $65M, put him on a personal allowance and required managers, controls and asset sales. New Jersey regulators put his total debt at $3.4 billion. The Taj Mahal went into a prepackaged bankruptcy in 1991, the Castle, the Plaza casino and the Plaza Hotel in 1992. The pattern of the next thirty years starts here: a crisis, a lender that will not let him fail, and the brand surviving the balance sheet.

"The banks extended his deadlines and provided a $65 million loan, but they also set a $450,000-a-month personal allowance and required new managers, financial controls, and asset sales."CNN, 2016 · cnn.com ↗ · Mother Jones timeline ↗ · the full record →

1998 AND 2015 · THE TAJ MAHAL AND FINCEN
FINCEN

A $10 MILLION PENALTY FOR "WILLFUL AND REPEATED" MONEY-LAUNDERING FAILURES, WITH CITATIONS BACK TO 2003

FinCEN fined the Taj $477,000 in 1998 for currency-reporting failures in 1990–91, then $10 million in 2015 for willful and repeated Bank Secrecy Act violations in 2010–12, noting examiner citations going back to 2003. The casino admitted it had no effective anti-money-laundering programme. This is the one place a federal regulator has formally found a Trump business failed to watch the money going through it.

"The casino admitted it lacked an effective anti-money-laundering program, did not report suspicious transactions, filed required currency transaction reports improperly, and kept inadequate records."FinCEN, 6 March 2015 · fincen.gov ↗ · the assessment PDF ↗

1998–2016 · BAYROCK AND FELIX SATER
RUSSIAN AND KAZAKH CAPITAL (ALLEGED)FBI COOPERATORTRUMP ORGANIZATION

A CONVICTED RACKETEER WITH A TRUMP ORGANIZATION BUSINESS CARD, A SOHO TOWER, AND A MOSCOW LETTER OF INTENT SIGNED MID-CAMPAIGN

Felix Sater pleaded guilty to racketeering in a $40M stock fraud in 1998 and became a federal cooperator. By 2006 he was at Bayrock, Trump's partner on Trump SoHo, where investors later alleged Kazakh money and the Icelandic FL Group put in $50M; those suits were allegations and settled. In 2010 he carried a Trump Organization card. The Mueller report records that Trump signed a Trump Tower Moscow letter of intent by November 2015 and that Michael Cohen spoke with the Kremlin press office about it in January 2016, while Trump was telling voters he had no business in Russia.

"Candidate Trump signed a Letter of Intent for Trump Tower Moscow by November 2015, and in January 2016 Trump Organization executive Michael Cohen emailed and spoke about the project with the office of Russian government press secretary Dmitry Peskov."Report on the Investigation into Russian Interference, vol. CRISIS WINDOW · JULY 2008 · THE PALM BEACH SALE
DMITRY RYBOLOVLEVDEUTSCHE BANK, IN COURT

BOUGHT FOR $41 MILLION OUT OF A BANKRUPTCY IN 2004, SOLD FOR $95 MILLION TO A RUSSIAN BILLIONAIRE TWO MONTHS BEFORE LEHMAN

The second window is the crash. Trump bought Maison de l'Amitié from a Chapter 7 estate in 2004, outbidding Jeffrey Epstein. In 2008, after renovations the Palm Beach Post called mostly cosmetic, courthouse records show it sold to Rybolovlev's family trust for $95 million; Lehman filed on 15 September. The same autumn Trump sued Deutsche Bank to escape a $640M Chicago loan, citing the crisis as force majeure, and the bank countersued on his $40M guarantee. Senators asked Treasury about the sale in 2018; both men say they have never met. The buyer later demolished the house and sold the lots for about $108M.

"Trump carried out renovations — mostly cosmetic — to the mansion before selling the estate in 2008 for what was then documented in courthouse records as $95 million to Russian billionaire Dmitry Rybolovlev."Palm Beach Post · via Dayton Daily News ↗ · CNN ↗ · the full record →

1998–2020 · DEUTSCHE BANK
DEUTSCHE PRIVATE BANK · VRABLICTHE MIRROR-TRADE BANK

THE BANK THAT MOVED $10 BILLION OUT OF RUSSIA WAS ALSO HIS LARGEST LENDER, OVER $2 BILLION

From 1998 Deutsche Bank lent him when, by his own suits' account, others would not: $125M for 40 Wall Street, $640M for Chicago, the Doral and the Old Post Office loans the New York court later found were obtained on inflated statements. Its private bank took over the relationship after the investment bank's 2008 litigation with him. The same institution paid $425M in 2017 for the Russian mirror trades and was never charged. He sued to keep Congress from the records and lost at every level. The record joins the two books of business only by the bank.

"Deutsche Bank has been the Trump Organization's biggest lender, financing more than $2 billion in loans to the president during his business career."CNBC, 3 November 2020 · cnbc.com ↗ · Trump v. Deutsche Bank, 2d Cir. ↗ · the full record →

2007–2018 · 666 FIFTH AVENUE
ANBANG (TALKS ENDED)QATAR (DENIES)BROOKFIELD

THE FAMILY'S OWN CRISIS: A $1.8 BILLION TOWER RESCUED BY A 99-YEAR LEASE FROM A FUND WHOSE SECOND-LARGEST INVESTOR WAS QATAR

Kushner Companies bought 666 Fifth at the top of the market in 2007, almost entirely with debt. In 2017, with Jared Kushner in the White House, the company negotiated with China's Anbang and with Qatar's former prime minister and finance minister; both tracks ended. In August 2018 Brookfield took a 99-year lease, reported as paid upfront at over a billion dollars, through a fund in which the Qatar Investment Authority held 9%. Brookfield and QIA both deny any Qatari role; the Senate Finance Committee asked Brookfield twice and was declined. The record here is denials and an unanswered question.

CRISIS WINDOW · 2017–2021 · THE PRESIDENCY AND THE PANDEMIC
CHINA ~$5.5MSAUDI ARABIAQATARUAEGSA · SBA

AT LEAST $7.8 MILLION FROM 20 FOREIGN GOVERNMENTS TO HIS PROPERTIES WHILE IN OFFICE; PPP MONEY TO HIS TENANTS; THE EMOLUMENTS SUITS DISMISSED AS MOOT THE WEEK HE LEFT

The third window is the office itself and the pandemic that ran through it. A House Oversight Democratic staff report, built on 451 pages of Mazars records, found at least $7.8M from 20 governments, China the largest. The emoluments suits brought by DC, Maryland and CREW reached the Supreme Court, which vacated them as moot five days after he left office, so no court ever ruled on the clause. PPP loans of more than $3.65M went to tenants at Trump and Kushner properties. The DC hotel lease was sold in 2022 for a reported $375M after $70M of losses. That is a staff report and reporting; it is the only accounting that exists.

"Democratic staffers on the House Oversight Committee on Thursday issued their 156-page report, and the payments came from foreign governments and government-backed entities from 20 countries."CBS News on "White House for Sale", 4 January 2024 · the report PDF ↗ · CBS ↗ · the full record →

2021–2026 · AFFINITY PARTNERS
PIF $2BQIALUNATEKUSHNER

SIX MONTHS AFTER OFFICE, SAUDI ARABIA'S FUND PUT $2 BILLION INTO THE SON-IN-LAW'S NEW FIRM OVER ITS OWN PANEL'S OBJECTION; $157.5 MILLION IN FEES SINCE

Jared Kushner ran Middle East policy in the first term and founded Affinity Partners in 2021. PIF's screening panel objected, citing the "inexperience" of the fund's management, an "excessive" fee and "public relations risks"; the full board under the crown prince overruled it. Qatar's and the UAE's funds followed. By 2024 the fund had collected $157.5M in management fees from foreign investors, $87M of it from PIF, with 99% of its capital foreign. It raised $1.5B more from QIA and Lunate in December 2024, a month after the election. Kushner says he would have raised the money "irrespective of the outcome." The senators' letters call it a compensation scheme; no body has ruled.

"Affinity has already collected approximately $157.5 million in management fees from foreign investors, including $87 million from the Saudi PIF."Senator Wyden to Affinity Partners, 24 September 2024 · finance.senate.gov ↗ · the panel's objections, via Middle East Eye ↗ · the full record →

2022–2025 · PEOPLE v. TRUMP
NEW YORK ATTORNEY GENERALDEUTSCHE BANK, AS THE LENDER DECEIVED

A COURT FINDS A DECADE OF INFLATED STATEMENTS TO LENDERS; THE APPEALS COURT THROWS OUT THE HALF-BILLION PENALTY AND KEEPS THE FINDINGS

This is the only full adjudication of how the money was raised. Justice Engoron found that from 2011 to 2021 Trump's statements of financial condition, submitted to Deutsche Bank and others, were inflated, ordered $354.9M in disgorgement plus interest, and barred him from running a New York business for three years. On 21 August 2025 the Appellate Division vacated the money as an excessive fine under the Eighth Amendment and left the liability findings and the injunctions standing; the lead opinion said the trial court "correctly found defendants liable." The lender whose deception the court found was the one on the Russian strand of this site.

"While the injunctive relief ordered by the court is well crafted to curb defendants' business culture, the court's disgorgement order, which directs that defendants pay nearly half a billion dollars to the state of New York, is an excessive fine."People v. Trump, 2025 NY Slip Op 04756, 21 August 2025 · nycourts.gov ↗ · the February 2024 decision ↗ · the full record →

A Trump family entity takes 75% of World Liberty's net revenue. On 23 October 2025 Trump pardoned Binance's founder, convicted in 2023, and said "I don't know who he is." His 2026 disclosure shows about $515M in tokens. The White House, Binance and Zhao all deny any deal. The Senate letter asks; no finding exists.

2024–2026 · THE GULF LICENSING INCOME AND THE ENVOY

His disclosures show about $21.9M in 2024 and $25.8M in 2025 from Dar Al Arkan and its London-listed arm Dar Global, the partner on the Jeddah, Dubai, Qatar, Maldives and Oman projects, plus a $5.25M Doha licence fee. Each item is a disclosure or a deed. None is a finding.

WHERE THAT LEAVES IT. Measured against this site's own definition, the record supports the conduit reading at two of the three windows and leaves the third open. In 1990 the banks carried him; in 2008 a Russian billionaire paid more than double for a house two months before the crash, while his own bank sued him and he sued it; and the only court that has examined how he borrowed found a decade of inflated statements to the Russian-strand bank. What the record does not do is turn the Gulf money since 2017 into a finding. The $7.8M, the $2B, the $157.5M in fees, the USD1 settlement, the pardon and the $25.8M a year are documents, not verdicts: the emoluments suits were mooted, the senators' letters are letters, and every counterparty denies a deal. A conduit is what the money did. Whether anyone was bought is a question the inquiries have, so far, declined to answer. Four ears.
