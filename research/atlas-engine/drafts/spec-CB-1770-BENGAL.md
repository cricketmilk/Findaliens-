## Historical anchors (established facts you may use)
- East India Company: private joint-stock company with monopoly on trade east of the Cape; after Plassey (1757) it collected the revenue (diwani) of Bengal, Bihar and Orissa.
- The Bengal famine of 1770 is estimated in contemporaneous Company and Indian sources to have killed roughly a third of the population of the affected provinces.
- The Company continued land-tax collection at high fixed demand during the famine years, and the price of rice and other grains rose dramatically in 1769-1770.
- The 1773 Regulating Act and the Tea Act followed: Parliament tightened control over the Company, granted a 1.4 million pound loan to relieve its debts, and left its monopoly intact - the loan was repaid through continued Bengal revenue.
- The 1784 India Act further subordinated the Company to a parliamentary Board of Control - the famine and the companys insolvency were cited as causes of the reform.
