## Historical anchors (established facts you may use)
- The War Department paid steamboat operators a per-head bounty to carry released Union prisoners north from Vicksburg in spring 1865 — five dollars an officer, ten dollars an enlisted man — which made a human payload the most valuable freight on the river.
- The Sultana was licensed for 376 passengers and left Vicksburg with roughly 2,100-2,400 aboard, most of them parolees from Cahaba and Andersonville.
- A boiler sprang a leak at Vicksburg; the boilermaker advised replacing the bulged sheets, and the repair actually made was a patch, because a days-long repair would have handed the payload to a rival boat.
- The boilers exploded above Memphis about 2 a.m. on 27 April 1865. Estimates of the dead run from about 1,100 to 1,800; it remains the deadliest maritime disaster in United States history.
- Captain J. Cass Mason died in the explosion. Captain Frederic Speed was court-martialled and convicted; the Judge Advocate General disapproved the verdict and it was annulled. Chief quartermaster Reuben Hatch, the officer suspected of the kickback, never appeared. Nobody was punished.
- The disaster competed for news with Lincoln’s assassination (14 April) and the killing of John Wilkes Booth (26 April) and was effectively buried.
