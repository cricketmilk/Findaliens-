## Historical anchors (established facts you may use)
- Titanic: White Star Line steamer, sank April 15 1912 after striking an iceberg on her maiden voyage; about 1,500 of roughly 2,200 aboard died.
- The steerage/third-class passenger death toll was proportionally far higher than first class; contemporary reports and later inquiries cited locked gates and passageway arrangements that hindered third-class access to the boat deck.
- The 1912-1913 US Senate inquiry (Smith Committee) and the British Wreck Commissioner inquiry (Mersey) investigated the loss; the Mersey inquiry has been criticized as a whitewash for its focus on crew conduct over company policy.
- J. Bruce Ismay, managing director of the White Star Lines parent company, survived the sinking and was widely condemned for leaving in a collapsible boat; the British inquiry declined to censure him.
- The disaster produced the 1913-1914 SOLAS convention mandating lifeboat capacity for all aboard - the first major international maritime safety treaty.
