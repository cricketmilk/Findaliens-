## Historical anchors (established facts you may use)
- The United Mine Workers struck Colorado Fuel & Iron — controlled by the Rockefeller interests — in September 1913; evicted miners and their families moved into tent colonies on leased ground, the largest at Ludlow.
- Company-paid guards and the Colorado National Guard faced the colonies through the winter. On 20 April 1914 the Ludlow colony was fired on and burned; about twenty people died, including two women and eleven children who suffocated in a pit dug beneath a tent.
- Two federal inquiries took evidence: the House Committee on Mines and Mining under House Resolution 387, and the U.S. Commission on Industrial Relations, which examined John D. Rockefeller Jr. in 1915 and published George P. West’s Report on the Colorado Strike; the Commission’s final report was filed as H. Rept. 64-31 on 11 January 1916.
- Militia officers tried by court-martial were acquitted; no one was convicted over the deaths.
- The company’s answer was the Colorado Industrial Plan — an employee-representation scheme drafted with W. L. Mackenzie King — and a publicity campaign run by Ivy Lee, whose bulletins were later shown to the Commission to be inaccurate.
