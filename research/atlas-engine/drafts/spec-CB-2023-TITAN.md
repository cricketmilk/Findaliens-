## Historical anchors (established facts you may use)
- On 18 June 2023, about 1047 local time, while diving to the wreck of the Titanic about 372 miles southeast of St. John's, Newfoundland and Labrador, the submersible Titan's pressure hull failed and the vessel imploded. All five persons on board died.
- The NTSB's Marine Investigation Report MIR-25-36 (investigation DCA23FM036) determines the probable cause to be "OceanGate's inadequate engineering process, which failed to establish the actual strength and durability of the Titan pressure vessel", with the hull having sustained delamination damage after dive 80 and further damage of unknown origin after dive 82, leading to a local buckling failure on dive 88. Contributing were "US and international voluntary guidance and US small passenger vessel regulations that were insufficient to ensure OceanGate adhered to established industry standards", and OceanGate's flawed analysis of its own pressure-vessel monitoring data.
- Titan was owned by Cyclops II Inc and operated by OceanGate Expeditions, based in The Bahamas, both affiliates of OceanGate Inc. The NTSB records that "The Titan was not registered in any US state, documented by the US National Vessel Documentation Center, nor inspected by any flag state", with flag, port of registry, IMO number and classification society all recorded as not applicable. Built 2020.
- OceanGate called its paying clients "mission specialists" — in the 2023 expedition's Project Execution Plan, "guests who help fund OceanGate" who were assigned tasks. A former operations technician told the chief executive "... you can't just change the title of a person when you're receiving compensation", and resigned citing "the legality of how it [OceanGate] was operating"; he also reported the CEO saying that "if the [US] Coast Guard became a problem ... he would buy himself a congressman and make it go away." The NTSB notes "OceanGate's intent to avoid safety oversight by assigning tasks to those paying to ride", while stating that it does not determine whether conduct violated statutory or regulatory requirements.
- When the Coast Guard published NVIC 05-93 in 1993, submersibles carrying six or fewer passengers were treated as "uninspected" vessels rather than small passenger vessels; after the Passenger Vessel Safety Act of 1993 the definition changed to include any submersible carrying at least one passenger for hire. OceanGate did not register or document the Titan in the United States or arrange Coast Guard inspection. NVIC 05-93 recommended keeping submersible operations within the depth at which rescue assistance is effective; the Titan regularly dove far beyond it, generally without rescue assistance standing by.
- The US Coast Guard Marine Board of Investigation released its Report of Investigation on 5 August 2025: more than 300 pages, 17 safety recommendations; chair Jason Neubauer said "This marine casualty and the loss of five lives was preventable." It found the primary contributing factors to be OceanGate's inadequate design, certification, maintenance and inspection process, and cited a toxic workplace culture, an inadequate regulatory framework and an ineffective whistleblower process under the Seaman's Protection Act. Its report was still under review by the Commandant, pending a Final Action Memorandum.
- The ROI PDF itself is served behind a bot wall and was not read; the Coast Guard citation is the official press release. Neither board determines liability, and no finding of liability should be implied.
