## Historical anchors (established facts you may use)
- Commissioner Lin Zexu confiscated and destroyed 20,283 chests of British-owned opium at Canton in 1839; Superintendent Charles Elliot had told the traders the Crown would make good their loss, converting a private contraband stock into a claim on the British Treasury.
- The Treaty of Nanking (29 August 1842) made the Chinese state pay that claim: Art. IV, six million dollars for the surrendered opium; Art. V, three million for Hong-merchant debts; Art. VI, twelve million for the expedition’s costs; Art. VII, twenty-one million dollars in all, on a schedule running to 1845, plus the cession of Hong Kong and five treaty ports.
- Sir James Graham’s censure motion of 7 April 1840 put the war to a three-night Commons debate (HC Deb 07 April 1840 vol 53 cc669-748); it was defeated 271 to 262, and the expedition sailed.
- Jardine, Matheson & Co. and Dent & Co. were the largest holders of the surrendered opium; William Jardine lobbied Palmerston in London through 1839-40 and supplied the war plan and the indemnity demand that the treaty substantially followed.
- Be conservative about attributions of motive to named individuals; the documented ledger is the treaty text and the Commons division, not private intent.
