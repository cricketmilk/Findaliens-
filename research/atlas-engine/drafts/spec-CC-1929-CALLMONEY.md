## Historical anchors (established facts you may use)
- Benjamin Strong was Governor (then styled Governor, later President) of the Federal Reserve Bank of New York from its founding in November 1914 until his death in October 1928.
- Montagu Norman was Governor of the Bank of England 1920–1944; the Strong–Norman working partnership, including shared views on gold-sterilisation and market management, is documented in scholarship and the FRBNY archives.
- At the July 1927 conference on Long Island, Strong, Norman, Rist and Schacht agreed to ease — a decision later identified (contested) as feeding the late-1920s speculation.
- The New York call-loan market financed stock margin purchases; broker loans roughly doubled 1927–1929 and non-bank lenders ("others") supplied most of the growth — all documented in Federal Reserve and NYSE data of the period.
- Strong’s death in October 1928 removed the transatlantic coordination; whether the NY Fed alone could manage the call-rate structure was the live question through the 1929 break.
