## Historical anchors (established facts you may use)
- The 1973–74 oil embargo roughly quadrupled the oil price; Saudi Arabia and other OPEC members accumulated large dollar surpluses with nowhere established to put them.
- Treasury Secretary William E. Simon led the 1974 Saudi negotiations that opened US Treasury purchases to the Saudis; the arrangement was secret at the time and reconstructed decades later from released official files and Simon’s own memoirs.
- The US-Saudi Joint Commission on Economic Cooperation (June 1974) institutionalised the financial and military relationship.
- The documented mechanism is surplus recycling through Treasuries and oil priced in dollars; the strong "secret treaty binding oil to the dollar" version is unsupported and must be separated from the documented recycling.
- The result locked in structural US fiscal capacity through the decade — OPEC surpluses became a standing bid under US debt, the ledger line the mechanism belongs on.
