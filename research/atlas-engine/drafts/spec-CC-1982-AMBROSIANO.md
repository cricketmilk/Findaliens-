## Historical anchors (established facts you may use)
- Banco Ambrosiano: Milan private bank known as the bankers' bank of the Vatican; its 1982 collapse (roughly $1.2-1.4bn in missing/phantom loans) was then Italy's largest banking failure.
- Roberto Calvi: chairman of Banco Ambrosiano, nicknamed 'God's Banker' for the Vatican's IOR shareholding and shell-company network; found dead, hanging under Blackfriars Bridge, London, June 18 1982 — days after fleeing a conviction for currency violations.
- Propaganda Due (P2): covert Masonic lodge led by Licio Gelli whose member list (discovered in 1981 raids) included politicians, bankers and officials; Gelli was a fugitive in South America in 1982.
- The Vatican's IOR (Institute for the Works of Religion) held an interest in Ambrosiano and was pressured into a settlement of roughly $250m for creditors after Calvi's death.
- Calvi's death was first ruled suicide by a London coroner's inquest; later Italian investigations and a second inquest concluded murder, and in 2005-2007 defendants including Giuseppe Calo and Flavio Carboni were acquitted of the murder at Rome trials.
