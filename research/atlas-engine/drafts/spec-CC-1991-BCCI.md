## Historical anchors (established facts you may use)
- Bank of Credit and Commerce International: founded 1972 by Agha Hasan Abedi, chartered in Luxembourg, run from London, with roots in Pakistan and financing from Gulf shareholders (ultimately the Abu Dhabi ruling family held a majority).
- Through the 1980s BCCI grew to ~70+ countries and laundered drug proceeds, arms money and dictator funds (Panama's Noriega, the Medellín cartel, Saddam Hussein's agricultural loans, Abu Nidal accounts). The Tampa undercover investigation ("Operation C-Chase") produced indictments of BCCI bankers in 1988-1990.
- BCCI secretly acquired U.S. banks (Financial General Bankshares, renamed First American) through fronts, with former Defense Secretary Clark Clifford as chairman and his partner Robert Altman as president — concealing foreign control in violation of U.S. banking law.
- July 5, 1991: the Bank of England and regulators in seven countries seized BCCI simultaneously. Indictments followed against BCCI Holdings (Luxembourg) S.A., Clifford and Altman (both later acquitted; Clifford's trial ended for health reasons). BCCI pleaded guilty in U.S. federal court (1992) to racketeering and money laundering.
- Roughly $10-13 billion vanished against roughly $20 billion in assets; depositors worldwide recovered cents on the dollar over decades of liquidation. The U.S. Senate "Kerry-Brown" report (1992, "The BCCI Affair") mapped the bank's intelligence-service entanglements.
- The DC District Court record cited here (1996-1999) is the aftermath: forfeiture and victims' asset-distribution litigation in United States v. BCCI Holdings.

## Decoy suggestion (adapt freely, do not invent facts)
- viralDecoy: "BCCI was a rogue outlier, a 'bank of crooks' that says nothing about respectable finance."
- whyDecoyFails: BCCI operated inside Washington through First American's blue-blood board, and moved money through the world's major clearing banks; the respectable letterhead was the mechanism.
- documentedLedger: the extraction ran through offshore chartering plus front-man acquisition of licensed U.S. banks — the black ledger was franchised through the front door.
