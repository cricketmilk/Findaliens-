## Historical anchors (established facts you may use)
- South Sea Company: chartered 1711 as a public-private monopoly on trade with Spanish South America in exchange for converting government annuity debt into company shares.
- The 1719-1720 debt-for-equity conversion scheme: holders of government annuities could exchange them for South Sea stock; the share price rose roughly tenfold in 1720 before the September crash.
- The Bubble Act (June 1720) restricted rival joint-stock promotion, concentrating speculation in South Sea stock; the 1721 Commons inquiry documented bribery of ministers and MPs via stock ledger entries.
- Robert Walpole survived the scandal; his 1721-1722 rise to lead the government was consolidated by his handling of the resolution — the episode was known as the Screen.
