## Historical anchors (established facts you may use)
- Samuel Plimsoll (1824-1898): English coal merchant turned MP for Derby, campaigner against overloaded ships.
- The British Merchant Shipping (Load Line) Act 1876 mandated a load line marking the maximum safe draught - the Plimsoll line - on British vessels.
- Plimsoll exposed shipowners who overloaded, insured heavily, and collected on sinkings; the phrase coffin ships was used for the practice.
- Plimsoll was briefly suspended from the Commons for disorderly protest against the watered-down bills before the 1876 Act passed.
- The load line became universal standard practice in merchant shipping; the mechanism is the industry profiting from loss of life (coffin ships) versus the marking that limited it.
