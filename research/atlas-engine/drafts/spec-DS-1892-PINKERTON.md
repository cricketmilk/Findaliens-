## Historical anchors (established facts you may use)
- Carnegie Steel’s chairman Henry Clay Frick locked out the Amalgamated Association at Homestead in July 1892 and contracted the Pinkerton National Detective Agency to deliver about 300 armed men by barge; the 6 July landing became a gun battle that killed men on both sides, and the Pennsylvania militia then occupied the town.
- Three congressional investigations followed: the House Judiciary Committee’s hearings (H. Misc. Doc. 52-335), its report of 7 February 1893 (H. Rept. 52-2447), and a Senate select committee moved by Senator Hansbrough from the Committee on Education and Labor (S. Rept. 52-1105, 1 August 1892).
- The statutory answer was the Anti-Pinkerton proviso carried in the sundry civil appropriations act of 3 March 1893 (27 Stat. 591, now 5 U.S.C. 3108): the federal government may not employ a Pinkerton employee or a similar organization. It binds federal hiring only — it says nothing about a private corporation hiring an armed agency against its own workforce.
- Prosecutions arising from Homestead — including treason charges against strike leaders — produced no convictions on either side.
- Be precise that the congressional records are investigations and a report, not a judgment of liability.
