## Historical anchors (established facts you may use)
- The Aldrich–Vreeland Act of May 1908, passed after the Panic of 1907, created the National Monetary Commission chaired by Senator Nelson W. Aldrich of Rhode Island.
- The commission toured Europe and issued its reports 1910–1912; its findings informed the Aldrich Plan for a National Reserve Association with 15 district associations under banker-dominated boards.
- In November 1910 Aldrich met a small group of banking figures at the Jekyll Island Club, Georgia, to draft the reserve plan — established by later accounts of participants, later itself framed by conspiracy writing as a secret cabal.
- The Aldrich Plan was rejected in Congress and attacked in the 1912 campaign as a "money trust" instrument; Woodrow Wilson ran against it.
- The Federal Reserve Act of December 1913 created the Federal Reserve System — a compromise adopting the reserve-association architecture but placing a public presidentially appointed Board over it.
