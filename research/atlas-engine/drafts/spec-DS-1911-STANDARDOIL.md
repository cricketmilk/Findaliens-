## Historical anchors (established facts you may use)
- Standard Oil Co. of New Jersey v. United States, 221 U.S. 1 (1911), on appeal from the Circuit Court for the Eastern District of Missouri: argued 14-16 March 1910, restored to the docket for reargument 11 April 1910, reargued 12-17 January 1911, decided 15 May 1911. The Court construed the Sherman Act "in the light of reason" so that it prohibits only unreasonable or undue restraints, and held the combination an unreasonable restraint of trade in petroleum.
- Paragraph 5 of the Circuit Court decree permitted "the distribution among the shareholders of the Standard Oil Company of New Jersey of the stocks held by that company" — the holding company was unwound into the hands of its own shareholders. This is recorded in the appellants' assignment of errors, which complained that the court then went on to limit the method of that distribution.
- The Supreme Court modified the decree in two respects that matter: it held "the delay of thirty days allowed for executing the decree was too short and should be extended so as to embrace a period of at least six months", and it held that the paragraph 7 injunction — which would have enjoined the New Jersey corporation and all the subsidiaries from carrying on interstate commerce until the stocks were transferred — "should not have been awarded".
- It construed paragraph 6 not as depriving the stockholders or corporations of the power "to make normal and lawful contracts or agreements", but "as restraining them from, by any device whatever, recreating directly or indirectly the illegal combination which the decree dissolved".
- Justice Harlan concurred in the general affirmance but dissented from the modification and from the rule of reason, writing that the Court had "read into the act of Congress words which are not to be found there".
- On 18 August 1914 William H. Libby, for the Standard Oil Company of New Jersey, wrote to the Secretary of State asking that the Government "secure the assent of the powers engaged in the present conflict to the exemption from seizure and inquiry of vessels transferred to the American flag which were actually owned in this country before the war began", citing the protection already accorded "the six American steamers of the International Mercantile Marine Company".
- Do not assert post-1911 ownership percentages or that any particular person retained control: the record read here shows what the decree permitted, not who ended up holding what. There is no documented Standard Oil gunboat episode in 1914-15 Mexico — the FRUS 1915 "Protection of American oil interests" file contains no occurrence of "Standard Oil" — so make no such claim.
