## Historical anchors (established facts you may use)
- Foreign Relations of the United States 1928, volume II, carries a chapter titled "Good offices of the Department of State to protect the interests of the United Fruit Company in Colombia" — four telegrams between the Secretary of State and the Minister in Colombia, March to June 1928, months before the strike.
- Roughly 25,000 workers in the Magdalena banana zone struck in November 1928. The demands were written contracts, a six-day week, an end to payment in company scrip, and medical care.
- On 6 December 1928 Colombian troops under General Carlos Cortés Vargas fired on a crowd gathered at the railway station square in Ciénaga. The death toll has never been settled: official Colombian figures were in the dozens, U.S. legation dispatches reported far higher numbers, and estimates range from under fifty to more than a thousand.
- Jorge Eliécer Gaitán’s 1929 debates in the Colombian Chamber of Representatives are the principal public reckoning; no U.S. or company officer faced any proceeding.
- The U.S. telegrams establish that the State Department acted as the company’s standing advocate in Bogotá. They do not themselves prove a U.S. instruction to fire — say so plainly rather than implying it.
