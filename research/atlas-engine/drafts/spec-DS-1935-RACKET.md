## Historical anchors (established facts you may use)
- This node is the racket thesis and Butler's own testimony. The 1933 Business Plot is a different matter and is already dossiered as "DuPont, General Motors & The Business Plot Axis" (CC-1914-DUPONT_GM_1934). Do not re-narrate the plot here.
- The Vinson-Trammell Act (Act of 27 March 1934, ch. 95, 48 Stat. 503, Public No. 135, H.R. 6604) required, as a condition of any naval vessel or aircraft contract, that the contractor agree to make a sworn report to the Secretary of the Navy on completion and "To pay into the Treasury profit ... in excess of 10 per centum of the total contract price". It also forbade subdividing contracts to evade the Act, opened the contractor's books to the Secretary of the Navy, the Secretary of the Treasury "and/or by a duly authorized committee of Congress", and required subcontractors to assent.
- The Senate Special Committee on Investigation of the Munitions Industry (the Nye Committee) reported in 1935 on naval shipbuilding that the Navy's failure to use its own yards as a yardstick "has resulted in leaving the profits of the shipbuilders practically uncontrolled"; that the Vinson-Trammell bill "limiting profits to 11.1 percent of cost cannot be enforced without a huge police force of accountants"; and that the Navy's 1933 and 1934 adjusted-price contracts "resulted, in effect, in cost-plus contracts", found "more profitable than the war-time contracts when only a 10-percent profit over cost was allowed".
- The two percentages are the same cap stated two ways: 10 percent of the contract price is 11.1 percent of the cost that makes up the other 90 percent. Say so rather than letting them look like a contradiction.
- The committee also found the Navy "has never examined the underlying costs or profits of the private builders", "makes no pretense of doing this", has no staff for it, and that the figures the committee studied "were all news to it"; enforcement was left "to the Treasury to do after 3 years, after a job is done". Evidence before it showed 1933 contracts worth $130,000,000 to private shipbuilders.
- Butler did not testify to the Nye Committee — two of its hearings volumes contain no occurrence of "Smedley". His own testimony that is on the record in this window is to the Senate Committee on Naval Affairs, on the 1914 "battle of El Tejar" near Veracruz: as recounted in the Senate on 5 March 1935, Butler testified that his troops reached the waterworks, made a reconnaissance and "could find no enemy", against General Russell's testimony of five or six hundred Mexicans and being shot at about twenty times. The chairman of that committee was Senator Park Trammell, whose name is on the 1934 profit cap. Do not stretch this into testimony about war profits; it is not.
- No verifiable 1935 scan of the book "War Is a Racket" was found, so do not quote the book.
