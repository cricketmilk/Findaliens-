## Historical anchors (established facts you may use)
- Blackwater guarded State Department personnel in Iraq under the Worldwide Personal Protective Services contract, a cost-reimbursement vehicle whose task orders ran to hundreds of millions of dollars; the House Committee on Oversight and Government Reform took testimony from Erik Prince at a hearing titled "Blackwater USA" on 2 October 2007.
- On 16 September 2007 Blackwater guards fired into traffic at Nisour Square in Baghdad, killing seventeen Iraqi civilians and wounding more.
- Coalition Provisional Authority Order 17 had placed contractors outside Iraqi legal process, so the only available forum was American. The criminal case was dismissed by the District Court for the District of Columbia on 31 December 2009 (United States v. Slough) over the use of the guards’ compelled statements; the D.C. Circuit revived it, and convictions followed years later in United States v. Slatten.
- The civil suits brought by victims’ families (Estate of Abtan v. Blackwater Lodge & Training Center) were settled; the four men convicted were pardoned by President Trump in December 2020.
- Keep the sequence exact — dismissal, revival, conviction, pardon — and do not state a settlement figure.
