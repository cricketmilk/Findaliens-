## Historical anchors (established facts you may use)
- On April 15, 1912, the first wireless bulletins relayed via shore stations and press associations reported the Titanic safe, in tow, all passengers saved; Halifax papers ran the false report the morning of April 16.
- The false dispatches traced to garbled relay traffic: wireless was a private commercial service (Marconi Company operators aboard ships) whose messages outran official confirmation; neither the White Star Line nor the government had a faster channel of its own.
- The fog persisted for hours; the first accurate passenger lists came only when the Carpathia docked in New York (April 18).
- The episode is a founding case of information fog in a mass-market event: the private information infrastructure produced the news, and the error corrected only when slower official channels caught up.
