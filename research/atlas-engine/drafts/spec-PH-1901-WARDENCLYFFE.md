## Historical anchors (established facts you may use)
- Nikola Tesla, financed by J. P. Morgan, began in 1901 to build a plant at Wardenclyffe (Shoreham, Long Island) intended for transatlantic wireless communication and, per Tesla's plans, ultimately global power transmission.
- Morgan had committed $150,000; the plant was unfinished when Marconi's transatlantic signal (December 1901) demonstrated wireless telegraphy on far less capital.
- Morgan declined further funding; Tesla mortgaged and lost the property, and the tower was demolished in 1917.
- The lore — Morgan withdrawing upon asking "where do we put the meter?" — is not documented; the documented record is the withdrawal of funding after a competitor's success and the loss of the plant.
- The United States Supreme Court in 1943 (Marconi Wireless Telegraph Co. v. United States, 320 U.S. 1) held that Tesla's prior patents anticipated parts of Marconi's patent.
