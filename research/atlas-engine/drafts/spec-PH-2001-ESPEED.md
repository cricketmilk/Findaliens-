## Historical anchors (established facts you may use)
- Cantor Fitzgerald: interdealer broker dominant in US Treasury trading; spun off its electronic trading arm eSpeed Inc. (IPO 1999, Nasdaq), which held patents on electronic matching of financial instruments.
- September 11, 2001: Cantor's offices in the North Tower (floors 101-105) destroyed; 658 employees killed — the largest loss of life by any single company. eSpeed lost many of its own staff.
- eSpeed v. BrokerTec USA (D. Del.): litigation over BrokerTec's electronic Treasury trading platform and eSpeed's matching patents, tried from 2004; the Federal Circuit ruled in March 2007 (480 F.3d 1129), addressing infringement and validity of eSpeed's patents. Use conservative language for holdings you are not certain of ("litigated", "contested").
- Cantor Fitzgerald & Co. v. American Airlines (S.D.N.Y. 2011): Cantor sued American over the 9/11 losses at the World Trade Center; the suit proceeded on negligence theories. Again, be conservative about the ultimate holding.
- Story arc: the firm that lost the most people on 9/11 simultaneously enforced a patent choke point over the electronic plumbing of the Treasury market — grief became leverage in the struggle over who owns the pipes of bond trading.
